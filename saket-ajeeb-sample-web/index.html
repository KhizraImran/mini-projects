Our Story

Saket Ajeeb opened in the 1970s in Khaitan and never changed. In a city that tears down and rebuilds every decade, this restaurant simply kept making chapati. No renovation. No rebrand. No Instagram. Just the counter, the fire, and the food.

The name holds its own poetry. Saket — meaning quiet and still, drawn from Sanskrit and Hindi. Ajeeb — meaning strange and wonderful, drawn from Arabic. Perhaps the founders knew something. The Wonderfully Strange. Fifty years later, Kuwaiti nationals, South Asian workers, and curious travellers all find their way here before the city wakes up.

There is no secret recipe for what makes Saket Ajeeb special. Fast service. Unbelievably cheap prices. Karak chai that actually tastes like someone cared. The kind of place that reviewers say you are out of the loop for never visiting. One of the oldest restaurants in Kuwait — and still the most honest.
